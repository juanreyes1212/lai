The wellness journal was the feature I was most likely to abandon — both
as a builder and as a user. A free-text "How are you feeling today?"
textarea is a great way to write nothing for six weeks straight. So the
journal in FitTrack is structured first, free-text second, and the
structure is doing most of the work.

## The shape of an entry

Every daily entry stores six things:

- `mood` — 1–5 scale, emoji-labeled
- `energy` — 1–5 scale, separate from mood (you can be happy and tired)
- `sleep_quality` — 1–5, distinct from the hours imported from the
  wearable
- `stress` — 1–5
- `wins` — a short text field, one line, prompt "What went right today?"
- `notes` — the open textarea, optional

Five sliders take fifteen seconds. The wins field is the one that
quietly does the heavy lifting — it forces a positive recap even on a
bad day, and three months in it's the most-read field when I scroll
backward.

## Why scales beat textareas

The point of a wellness journal isn't journaling — it's *trend data*.
A five-point mood scale plotted across 90 days tells you something. 90
paragraphs of "felt okay I guess" tell you nothing you can act on.

The scales also let me overlay the journal on the same chart as HRV,
sleep duration, and training load. Patterns show up that no single
metric would: HRV tanks for three days, then mood follows. Sleep
quality (self-rated) drifts down a week before sleep duration does.
The journal is the *subjective* signal layered on top of the
*objective* wearable data, and they only become useful together.

## The streak that isn't a streak

There's no streak counter. I tried one for a week and removed it. The
moment you gamify journaling, the entries get shorter and more
dishonest — people log a 3/3/3/3 just to keep the flame icon alive.

Instead the dashboard shows "27 of the last 30 days logged" as a quiet
stat, and missing a day costs nothing. The goal is honest data, not a
high score.

## Prompts that rotate

The textarea has a placeholder that rotates daily from a list of about
twenty prompts:

- "One thing you're carrying that you could put down."
- "What did your body ask for today that you ignored?"
- "Something a past version of you would be proud of."
- "What's one thing you want to remember about today?"

A blank textarea is intimidating. A textarea with a specific question
in light gray text is a conversation starter. The user can ignore it
and write whatever — but most days, they don't.

## What the AI does with it

The same OpenAI layer that adjusts macros also reads the last 7 days
of journal entries when generating the morning recommendation. Low
mood + low HRV + bad sleep quality for three days running flips the
recommendation tone from "push harder on protein" to "today is a
recovery day — eat to maintenance, get to bed early." That's the
payoff for collecting the subjective data. It's not a diary; it's an
input.

## Privacy

The journal entries never leave the database unless the AI call is
made, and even then only the last 7 rows go in the prompt — no
historical lookback, no training data export, no third-party
analytics. The whole point of a private fitness tracker is that the
sensitive stuff *stays* private. Journaling is the most sensitive
field, so it gets the strictest scope.
